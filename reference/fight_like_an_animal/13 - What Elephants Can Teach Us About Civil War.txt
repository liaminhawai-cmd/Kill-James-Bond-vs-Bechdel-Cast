Despite the impact of films like The Matrix on mass culture, quips about the universe being a simulation on the internet were relatively uncommon until 2020, when their incidents began to sharply trend upward, a year when suicide also skyrocketed.
2020 was a year when the distinct effects isolation has on the human mind began to be felt at a new scale in an already lonely civilization.
This begets the question of whether the emergence of simulant subculture is the result of a particular type of disassociation that occurs in a hyper-technological milieu.
Feeling like nothing is real is nothing new. Perhaps what is most novel about simulance is just their ability to reinforce the perceptual distortions produced by traumatic disassociation by forming online communities.
Certainly, this would not be the only case where people formed an internet subculture and a corresponding cosmology based on a shared mental health issue.
The targeted individual community is probably the earliest unequivocal example. Targeted individuals found each other on the internet and began comparing notes on the attempts at mind control they were experiencing and the clandestinely placed implants their bodies contained,
forming not only a support community, but a full-fledged internet culture with media projects and celebrities.
That these people were probably all sharing the same set of psychiatric symptoms, and that is why sharing notes on their experience produced such convergent scenarios is a fairly easy conclusion for most people not subject to these particular afflictions to come to.
But if you watch the 2017 Vice Documentary Meet the Targeted Individual Community, you see that there is some genuine comfort being taken by these people and having formed a community that validates their experience.
Trying to fight off what others would call delusions and tether one's mind to some approximation of a consensus version of reality carries one set of burdens and plausibilities.
Deciding to embrace what others would call delusions and talk about them with people who are experiencing something similar carries another.
