At the time we received, to our profound surprise, half of the budget we wrote in 2024 from a
single funder, it could be said that no strategy for survival existed in the following sense.
There was no set of behaviors, structures, or social processes, which we were trying
to convince people to adopt or create that were tangibly modeled anywhere.
Whatever a revolution consisted of, we were expecting an awful lot of its concrete details
to be inferred from far more abstract principles, and these principles were mostly articulated
in text.
This was a problem as about half of the people in the United States couldn't even
really read beyond a grade school level.
A very small proportion of the other half seemed to have any concrete sense of how any political
doctrine translated into a set of actions which could be carried out in their immediate context.
So we tried to illustrate various elements of our socio-political program with very clear,
redirect stories.
Take 2028's patrolling the wasteland.
It was a story about life in an already destitute rural area as industrial civilization collapses,
or in any case, contracts sufficiently that basic necessities are no longer available.
It illustrates the core principle of organizing multiple forces capable of using violence
for the purpose of preventing any one of them from gaining despotic power.
The Cosmatoria runs deep throughout the film.
A romantic relationship the audience is encouraged to become invested in falls apart as the
woman finds the man's artistic eccentricities and lack of practical abilities increasingly
frustrating in the new reality they inhabit.
A child gets killed trying to steal desperately needed food for the family that has taken him
in.
All the markers of a Hollywood story with a happy ending are there.
Many cinematic devices are employed to portray the child as the embodiment of the human
spirit overcoming the odds, but it ends tragically because it was statistically far
more likely to end tragically than otherwise.
There's even a part where a family eats a horse they are deeply attached to.
But along the way, we learn a great deal about the fundamental principle of multiple
militias existing in parallel to one another, with individuals participating in multiple militias
so that no one of them gains despotic power, and the plausible threat of counter-aggression
prevents most actual aggression from occurring.
Rural America is a profoundly culturally heterogeneous place, and patrolling the wasteland is emphatically
not a story about everyone coming together in any broad sense.
It is a story about very different people, with as radically disparate ways of experiencing
the world as one could possibly hope to find, allying with one another in such a way
that no one faction ends up in charge of everyone else.
The crazy quilt nature of human experience is a down-to-earth experience.
