# Essay 09 (composite) — The preview pairs: Spice World, Romy and Michele, The Birdcage, What Women Want, Sex and the City
## What the asymmetry encodes, and what survives a ten-minute window

The five remaining pairs cannot be analysed like the eight before them, and the reason they can't is itself the most important thing about them. For every one of these films — all five feminine-coded — Kill James Bond exists only as a roughly ten-minute free Patreon preview, the loss-leader trailer for a paywalled bonus episode, while The Bechdel Cast covered each as a full main-feed episode. The data is structurally lopsided: ninety minutes of BC against ten of KJB, and the ten are KJB's *off-format* register, the "girls night" sleepover episode rather than the main-feed show. The brief's methodological rule is therefore strict and correct — matched-window register analysis only, KJB's preview against BC's first ten minutes, and no full-episode claims. Treated that way, the five pairs do three things at once: they make the programming asymmetry legible as a finding, they let the orthogonality hypothesis survive its hardest and shortest test, and they show both machines being fed their richest fuel without either changing gear.

### The asymmetry is the finding — but read it carefully

Lay out the corpus by format and the pattern is total: every masculine-coded film KJB covered — Bond, the heists, the action canon — is a free main-feed episode; every feminine-coded film — the Spice Girls, two 90s women's comedies, a drag farce, a rom-com, a Carrie-Bradshaw movie — is a paid bonus, released to the main feed only as a ten-minute preview. The free/paid boundary in KJB's catalogue runs exactly along the gender-coding of the source film. That is a real structural fact and it does encode something about the imagined listener: the default, free, on-format show is the one that interrogates "boy movies," and the women's films are excursions.

But the honest reading is more interesting than "the women's films are marginalised," and the previews themselves supply the correction, because KJB is conspicuously self-aware about the whole arrangement. The What Women Want preview names the show's own premise precisely: "a big part of Kill James Bond as a podcast has been us exploring what it means to like what you might reductively call boy movies — or, as they used to be called, *movies*." The asymmetry is downstream of the show's origin (it began as a Bond show; the masculine-default canon *is* its remit, by construction), not a verdict on whether women's films deserve coverage. And the affective framing of the bonus episodes is the opposite of marginalisation — they are *treats*: "girls night," "the most girls night it's ever been," the sleepover, the Pride-month pick, the nail-painting bit. The feminine-coded film is the show's reward to itself, the off-format indulgence, framed as celebration. So what the free/paid line encodes is not contempt for the women's film but the structure of a show built around the masculine-default canon, which experiences the women's film as a holiday from its own format — and says so. The discipline the brief asked for holds: note the asymmetry, don't over-read it, and let KJB's own self-description do the disambiguating.

### Orthogonality survives the ten-minute window

If the register were going to break anywhere, it would break here: on the most feminine-coded films in the corpus, in KJB's off-format "girls night" mode, in a ten-minute window with no time to settle. It doesn't break. The windowed counts (regenerable from the tagging pipeline, matched to each preview's word length against BC's opening) show KJB's profanity running structural — 5 to 7 per thousand words on Spice World, Romy, the Birdcage, SATC — against BC's punctuational near-zero, the same constitutive-versus-marked split the main-feed essays found. And the systems engine is fully intact in miniature: the Spice World preview reads the Spice Girls as a *state* phenomenon — "a deep-state project to create a girl group that would unify the nation," "critical national infrastructure," New Labour's answer to the CIA's mind-control programmes — the exact object/system register KJB runs on Bond's watches and the heist films' economics, now aimed at a pop group. The framework-building (H17c) survives the window too: the Romy preview mints "girls is when you're colourful, masculinity is when you're black and white, as we learned from the card counter" (a transferable colour-coding-of-gender thesis, citing the show's own prior episode), and the What Women Want preview builds an argument about the unheralded by-and-for-women romance-novel canon and reframes "Professor X, but only for chicks — Professor XX." Even in ten off-format minutes, KJB legislates frameworks. (The one apparent wobble — the What Women Want window showing zero profanity and a very high hedge rate — is not a register break but small-window noise over a Devon-less two-hander in exactly the reflective theory-construction mode that drives hedges up, the H2 second mechanism; the engine underneath is unmistakably KJB.)

H1 therefore holds at the extreme, in the shortest possible window, in the off-format mode — which is the strongest available statement of it. The register is stable not just across the film's gender-coding and the show's own politics but across its own free/paid format boundary.

### Both machines, richest fuel, same gear: the interaction effect on both sides

The brief predicted an interaction effect — that topic feeds each machine different *fuel* without flipping either, and that BC's autobiography channel "amplifies enormously on childhood-nostalgia films." These five films are precisely that nostalgia material (Spice World, Romy, SATC especially), and the prediction is confirmed quantitatively on the BC side and extended to a parallel on the KJB side.

On BC, the autobiography channel does not just amplify, it spikes to corpus-record levels: first-person-singular rates in the matched windows run 58 (Birdcage), 61 (Spice World), 68 (SATC), 77 (What Women Want) per thousand words — every one of them above BC's rate on any masculine-coded film (which ran 24 to 46). The feminine-coded nostalgia film is the maximal trigger for BC's I-and-me register, the show routing the film through personal memory more densely than anywhere else in the corpus. The machine runs hotter; it does not change.

And on KJB the same interaction appears in its own idiom — not autobiography but *identification*, and this is the composite's genuine discovery. On the masculine-coded films, KJB's approach-grammar points outward as prosecution (the named men, the institutions, the "God's gift to bullies"). On the feminine-coded films it points the same energy *inward and toward desire* — longing, recognition, wish-fulfilment. The Romy preview is the clearest specimen: "this is the ideal existence, this is what I wanted, to be a twenty-something blonde American woman in Venice Beach doing absolutely nothing… this is all I've ever wanted," and the film read flatly as trans text ("it's about being transgender — because Eleven hadn't been invented yet, it has to be," "AGP as fuck, this is the whole movie"). The Birdcage preview runs a careful, inside reading of where drag and trans identity had and hadn't separated by 1996; the Spice World preview opens on "girls night, the most girls night it's ever been." The affect *target* flips — from attacking the masculine film to identifying with the feminine one — while the register (systems engine, structural profanity, framework-minting, initiate-address) stays exactly itself. This is H6 and H16 confirmed from the other side: the same inside-the-position identification that read the Bond films from the harmed position reads the women's films from the longed-for position, and the approach-grammar that prosecutes masculine films *celebrates* feminine ones. Both shows, handed their richest fuel, run hotter in the same gear: BC's autobiography maxes out, KJB's identification flips from prosecution to longing, and neither machine's register moves.

### What can and can't be said about convergence here

The asymmetric data forbids the H9 full-episode convergence claims the main essays could make — ten minutes of KJB cannot be set against ninety of BC as equals, and the brief rightly bars it. What the windows do show, where the same early material is covered, is the same rhyme: both shows open Spice World on the band-as-phenomenon and the manufactured-girl-power question; both treat the Birdcage's drag/identity politics as the live issue; both read What Women Want's premise (a misogynist gains telepathic access to women) as the obvious satirical hook and both flag the Nancy Meyers female-director fact. The conclusions, in the overlapping windows, point the same way they have all along. But this is corroboration, not proof, and it is logged as such: the preview pairs *support* the convergence thesis at the level of the opening window and *cannot extend* it to the whole episode. The eight full-length pairs carry the H9 weight; these five add a consistent but window-bounded fifth-decimal-place agreement.

### What the composite settles

It settles the orthogonality hypothesis in its strongest form: KJB's register survives the most feminine-coded films, the off-format "girls night" mode, and a ten-minute window, intact down to the profanity rate and the systems engine. It confirms the brief's predicted interaction effect, and improves on it — BC's autobiography channel spikes to corpus-record first-person rates on the nostalgia films, and KJB shows the mirror-image interaction the brief didn't predict: its inside-the-position identification flips from prosecution (masculine films) to longing (feminine films) while the machine holds its gear. And it converts the programming asymmetry from a curiosity into a finding with a disciplined reading: the free/paid boundary runs along gender-coding because the show is built on the masculine-default canon and experiences the women's film as a self-aware holiday from its own format — a treat, not a marginalisation, and KJB says so itself.

With the five preview pairs folded in, the corpus is complete: eight full-length pairs carrying the hypothesis stack, five preview pairs corroborating the register signal at the window level and supplying the programming-asymmetry finding. The scoreboard has stopped moving. What remains is the synthesis — the three throughline chapters the skeletons have been accumulating toward (the two economies and the confound; convergent instruments and divergent stances; status, positionality, and the Schröder reading of the hosts) plus the methods-and-caveats chapter that the whole project's discipline has been writing in the margins all along.
