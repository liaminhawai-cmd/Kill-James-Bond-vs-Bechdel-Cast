# Essay 03 — Austin Powers: International Man of Mystery (1997)
## "You are the in-group that I bind" / "You got to be smarter than what you're trying to make fun of"

### 1. The pair

**Kill James Bond!**, S2E23, the first night of a three-night live run at a Streatham venue (nights two and three — *The Spy Who Shagged Me* and *Goldmember* — are in the corpus as off-corpus extras). Datable to spring 2023: the hosts compare scores with the Atomic Blonde episode "like it came out yesterday" (S2E22, April 2023), and the topical texture (Liz Truss, Andrew Tate's Romania arrest, "we had to wait for China to reinvent polyamory") agrees. About 101 minutes. **The live-format confound the brief flags is not a nuisance on this pair — it is the finding**, as §3 will argue.

**The Bechdel Cast**, with returning guest Atsuko Okatsuka, datable precisely to April 2020: recorded in early-lockdown quarantine, and the episode exists *because* the new Bond film was pulled from its April release ("all of our plans have fallen to shit") — Austin Powers was the substitute. About 76 minutes net of an unusually intrusive ad load. Era skew exactly three years.

Guest symmetry continues, with a twist: Okatsuka is the corpus's first guest who is *structurally an outsider* to the material — she grew up outside the US, never found Myers funny, and frames her whole relationship to the film as "I thought it was me… it's because I didn't grow up here." The hosts' response — "no, something's wrong with Mike Myers and something's wrong with the culture" — is the BC validation subroutine doing exactly what the framework predicts, and her outsider position generates the episode's best analysis (below).

One housekeeping note: the KJB ASR on this file is rough even by corpus standards (live audio, audience noise, several echo-loop artifacts), and one long passage collapses entirely. Nothing below rests on a single line from those regions.

### 2. The headline finding: the live show as a natural experiment on the format confound

The brief's confound table ranks the candidate explanations for the two shows' differences: **format/production ≥ nationality > gender-positionality > openness > neurodivergence**. That ranking has so far been a structured bet. This episode is the closest thing the corpus will ever give us to an *experiment* on it — because for one night, KJB's format variable moves. The studio trio acquires what BC always has: a present, reactive audience. If the register differences are mostly format, KJB's pragmatics should drift BC-ward under live conditions. They do, in two measurable ways.

**Apology tokens invert.** In The Rock, apologies ran 11–2 in BC's favour, and the corpus pattern has been BC-repairs / KJB-never-repairs. Here KJB produces *thirty* apology tokens (1.8/1k words) against BC's four. Hand-checking confirms these are genuine floor-management repairs, not mock-apology: hosts apologising to each other for talking over drops, for "hostile people management on stage," for the crack about a co-host's notes ("I'm sorry, I said the thing about your notes — fuck you — I deserved it"). With an audience present, KJB's staged combat suddenly requires the on-mic repair traffic that BC's format has always required.

**Self-competence deflation appears in KJB.** Essay 01 sharpened H4 to: both shows criticise outward, but only BC sacrifices its own competence. On stage, KJB sacrifices its own competence repeatedly — "all my notes were dog shit," the running bit of the audience out-remembering the hosts on their own SCUM database ("not only am I getting the shit kicked out of me… but the audience is scoring points off the…"). The no-self-deflation rule, solid across two studio episodes, bends the moment there is a room to lose face in front of.

What does *not* move is just as important. The theory-of-the-listener asymmetry (H2) **intensifies** rather than converging: far from onboarding anyone, the live KJB show contains the corpus's most explicit statement of initiate-address ever recorded — "for the next hour and a half we're going to be teaching you about the concepts of in-groups and out-groups," followed by "you are the in-group that I bind" to the room and a cheerful "if you're listening to us after the fact, fuck off" to the at-home audience. The hedging gap also persists (BC 10.8 vs KJB 5.8 per 1k — 1.9:1, between The Rock's 1.4 and Spice World's extreme).

So the natural experiment returns a split result with a clean interpretation: **the pragmatics of repair and self-deflation are substantially format-driven** (add an audience and KJB drifts BC-ward within one evening), while **audience-theory is constitutive** (the in-group boundary is not a format accident; it is the show's identity, and a live room makes it *louder*). This is direct evidence for the confound table's top ranking — format owns more of H4 than previously credited — while carving out what format cannot explain. H4's scoreboard entry needs a format-sensitivity caveat, and the synthesis chapter on the confound problem just acquired its best exhibit.

### 3. An honest miss: H3's lexicon fails to dissociate here

The disgust/anger counts that produced clean double dissociations on The Rock (12–0 / 8–2) and Casino Royale (15–3) **do not replicate on this pair**: KJB raw disgust 13, BC 8 (0.8 vs 0.6 per 1k — the wrong direction for the hypothesis, since here KJB scores *higher*), anger negligible on both sides. Two things are true at once. First, hand-checking rescues some of the construct: KJB's disgust-lexicon hits are mostly stance-frames rather than recoil ("I *hate* defending this movie" is an argumentative move, not an enforcement affect), and the qualitative grammars still differ (BC's verdict vocabulary is "despicable… excruciating to watch… bad, bad, bad"; KJB's is prosecution of named men — Myers's power over the script, the Joe Son record). Second, the quantitative claim as stated in essays 01–02 was too strong. Exactly as open question 12 anticipated, the lexical signal is partly an artefact of how much desire/body material the film supplies (Casino Royale maximally; Austin Powers, oddly, less — its sex is cartoon sex), and partly swamped here by the live format, which converts KJB's enforcement into audience-facing bit-prosecution that my small lexicons don't catch. H3 is hereby demoted from "robust lexical dissociation" to "robust *functional* difference whose lexical shadow is pair-variable." Function-coding on hand-checked samples, not raw lexicon counts, must carry the claim from here on. Logged in FINDINGS as the essay's primary correction-adjacent result.

### 4. The strata that yield

**Discourse architecture — instrument elasticity, symmetric specimen of the corpus.** Both shows bend their scoring instruments past their own bounds on this pair, in opposite directions, for the same expressive reason. KJB's SCUM run scores Smarm 7, Cultural insensitivity 7 ("Live and Let Die was an eight" — the cross-episode database consulted on stage, the audience out-remembering the hosts), Unprovoked violence 2, and Misogyny **8 — on a 0-to-7 scale**, total 24 "by the standards of the podcast pretty bad," against the all-time worst of ~28. BC's guest awards the film a **"concave nipple… a negative one"** — below the scale's zero floor, with a purity-flavoured disclaimer attached even in the joke ("not to body shame, but because it's like a negative nipple"). Two rubrics, both broken downward through their floors and upward through their ceilings in the same analytic week, because the object under measurement exceeded the instrument. For H8 this is the best evidence yet that *both* shows treat their instruments as elastic expressive devices rather than fixed metrics — the difference remaining exactly where the corrected H8 put it: stance. KJB breaks the scale deadpan, inside the bit, with database continuity maintained; BC's guest breaks it with a care-disclaimer and the hosts ratify it warmly.

And the H14 census advances with a death notice: **GIRTH does not appear**. Minted in The Rock (episode 22, ~Dec 2021) with cross-episode comparability promised, the instrument is absent from this S2 episode despite Austin Powers being precisely the "assigned movies at birth" material GIRTH was built for — instead SCUM takes jurisdiction over the Bond-parody. No Kronsteen Rosette, Kaufman Star, or Goodnight Cross is administered either (the live format compresses the apparatus to SCUM alone, though ASR loss can't be fully excluded). Meanwhile the lore that *does* persist is telling: the Spectre "metal plates" bit runs unglossed, the Man from Hong Kong / Grand Unified Theory of Bonds gets a callback eighteen months after The Rock minted it, "historical allegations" (Lazenby) likewise. The ratchet, on this evidence, is selective: *bits* with affective resonance survive and compound; *procedures* survive only if they keep getting jurisdiction. Instrument mortality is itself H14 data, and open question 6 is half-answered — GIRTH did not recur at its first obvious opportunity. (The S4 episodes will settle whether it ever resurfaces.)

**Semantics and pragmatics — the same conclusion, two grammars, again.** The H9 convergence engine produces its most nearly verbatim output yet. BC: "remember how horribly the Bond films treated women — let's do the same exact thing 30 years later," plus the pedagogical apparatus: Caitlin's parody/satire distinction — **which is the brief's §4 specimen of BC defining-terms-as-teaching, confirmed here word for word** ("parody is… doing a similar version… satire is actual possible social commentary") — and the verdict "you got to be smarter than what you're trying to make fun of." KJB, independently, two registers over: "if you just do it again in the 90s as a joke, that's still doing it again. That's the same thing," and on the fembots, "they treat them like robots and then do it as well — you can't have the two different pastiches." The *doing-it-again-is-still-doing-it* thesis is reached by both shows with no possible cross-contamination (2020 vs 2023), down to the same supporting exhibits: Will Ferrell's brownface, the Japan fixation, the disposable women. Both shows also run *production-economics* explanations of the franchise's decline — BC with cited budget figures, gross totals, and the Princess Diana release-window; KJB with a power-theory ("as it made more money, people stopped being able to say no to Mike Myers") capped by an authorship theory built from reading the shooting script: the Carrie Fisher script-doctor hypothesis, KJB's purest theory-vs-catalogue specimen on the pair (uncited primary-source research assembled into a legislated explanation; BC's equivalent industrial analysis — the Jay Roach résumé-recycling argument, Austin Powers to *Bombshell* — is scaffolded on a named GQ piece and runs through a guest-validated moral frame). H7 needs an honest footnote here: BC manifestly *can* run systems analysis when the system is Hollywood-institutional; the engines differ in their home domains (state/military/economic vs industry/representation) more than in raw capacity.

**The identification axis at its sharpest (H6).** This pair contains the corpus's most direct positional mirror, because both shows analyse *the same two transphobic punch scenes*. BC reads them from outside, protectively, as harm done to characters and audiences: "she gets punched in the face, she gets called a man… that was so upsetting," with the worst part being "it's a callback." KJB reads them from inside the blast radius: "we know that line because **we've had it yelled at us out of cars**," followed by the bleakest self-positioning joke in the corpus — the film as "God's gift to bullies," and "as a trans person, you have to sit yourself down and go: okay, first thing, our bullies were right." And the positional difference licenses different *analytic* moves: KJB can afford to complicate ("this joke is less transphobic in context than I remember — it's a lift from Thunderball… but no one remembers that, and the kids remembered the line"), where BC, speaking from outside, condemns plainly and cannot risk appearing to excuse. Same scenes, same verdict, opposite epistemic permissions. This is H6 and H11 fused, and it is the specimen I'd put first in any synthesis chapter on positionality.

**H16 stress test — passed, with a doubled warrant and a logged impurity.** Open question 10 asked whether this pair would break the affect-grammar mapping. It doesn't, and it adds something better than survival: **BC independently produces its own folk version of the reactive-aggression construct.** Okatsuka and the hosts, riffing on the judo-chop and the Scientology location, build a genuine micro-theory: "everything about this movie is… very incel… it's always evil dudes who are angry about not getting laid" who "get really into martial arts" — aggression as compensation for a threatened self-image, which is Schröder's reactive-aggression profile stated in vernacular. So both shows now hold folk versions of the distinction: KJB uses it to *score* violence (Casino Royale's "it's not reactive violence… it's quite thought out"), BC uses it to *diagnose* a masculinity. The lens keeps being licensed by the corpus itself. The logged impurity: KJB's vocabulary here includes contamination items ("rancid 90s irony misogyny," "grim") — the grammars are dominant tendencies, not exclusive territories, and FINDINGS should say so.

### 5. Hypothesis scorecard

**H1: supported ×8.** Live format, lockdown format, three-year skew — neither register flips.
**H2: supported directionally (1.9:1); theory-of-listener now ×3 and strengthened** — the in-group/out-group lecture is the explicit constitutional statement of initiate-address.
**H3: COMPLICATED — lexical dissociation fails to replicate** (0.8 vs 0.6/1k). Demoted to a functional claim requiring hand-coded samples; Q12's artefact worry partially confirmed. Primary honest miss of the essay.
**H4: COMPLICATED, productively — format-sensitive.** Apology tokens invert (30–4 KJB); self-competence deflation appears in KJB under live conditions. The diagnostic holds for studio episodes only; the live drift is direct evidence for format's top rank in the confound table.
**H5: supported.** The "feminism inspector" bit — KJB ironizing its own enforcement role while performing it earnestly — joins the GIRTH and SCUM specimens; BC's "now that's what I call feminism, volume 1997" snap-back likewise.
**H6 (with H11): strongest mirror in the corpus** — the same transphobic scenes read from outside (protective condemnation) and inside (lived target, textual complication licensed by position).
**H7: supported with an honest footnote.** BC runs institutional-industrial systems analysis (Roach, budgets) capably; home domains differ, raw capacity doesn't.
**H8: enriched — instrument elasticity is symmetric** (8/7 vs the concave nipple); stance remains the differentiator.
**H9: strongest verbatim convergence yet** — doing-it-again-is-still-doing-it, reached independently three years apart, with matching exhibits.
**H12: supported.** Script-as-primary-source theorising vs GQ/figures scaffolding.
**H13: supported.** Okatsuka's quarantine disclosure ("I've lost the ability to form sentences… I haven't seen people") is biography-as-rapport; KJB's disclosures (judo childhood, bullying, the cars) are text-routed and analytic without exception.
**H14: census advanced — first instrument death recorded** (GIRTH absent at its first natural jurisdiction), lore persistence confirmed (metal plates, GUTB callback), apparatus compresses to SCUM under live conditions.
**H16: stress test passed; warrant doubled** (BC's folk reactive-aggression theory); impurity logged (KJB contamination vocabulary exists).
**H15: held.** The KJB hosts' bullying and street-harassment disclosures are used here solely as H6 positional evidence, exactly as the rule requires.

### 6. What this pair adds

First, **the natural experiment**: one night of audience-facing KJB moves the repair and self-deflation pragmatics measurably BC-ward while leaving audience-theory untouched — the cleanest evidence yet for the confound table's format-first ranking, and a ready-made centrepiece for the synthesis chapter on what n=2 can and can't decompose.

Second, **the symmetric scale-breaking**: 8-out-of-7 and the concave nipple, the same week in analytic terms. Both instruments are elastic; only the stance differs. H8's corrected formulation survives its best test.

Third, **discipline from the data**: H3's lexical version is dead as a universal claim; long live the functional version. An essay series that only ever confirms itself isn't measuring anything.

Fourth, **GIRTH's death notice** — the first negative entry in the H14 apparatus ledger, which converts the lore-ratchet question from "does apparatus accumulate?" to the sharper "what *selects* which apparatus survives?" Current answer sketch: affective bits outlive procedures; jurisdiction is use.

What would disconfirm this essay's claims: a studio-format KJB episode with high genuine-repair traffic (would break the format reading of H4 — the S4 episodes can test this); GIRTH resurfacing in S4 (would void the death notice — check Point Break and F&F); a pair where BC's and KJB's *conclusions* genuinely diverge (H9's standing challenge, still unmet after three essays).
